## Problem Statement
The gender pay gap remains a significant social and economic issue in the UK, affecting equality, workforce participation, and economic policy. 


## Objectives  
1. Analyze gender pay gap statistics using official UK data.  
2. Break down the pay gap by:
- Regions in UK
- Gender
- Company Size
- Sectors
  

## Dataset Overview  
- [Official Data Source](https://gender-pay-gap.service.gov.uk/)

- [Dataset](https://gender-pay-gap.service.gov.uk/viewing/download)
- [Data Dictionary](https://www.bbc.com/news/business-47754005)


## Recommendations
- Employers should review pay practices, especially in industries or roles with large historical gaps.  
- Increase transparency: public reporting, pay bands, and bonus structures can help reduce disparities.  
- Introduce or strengthen mentorship and promotion programs to support women in reaching higher pay quartiles.  
- Encourage policy interventions (e.g., pay disclosures, audits) focused on sectors with the highest pay gap.

## Limitations
- The data does not necessarily reveal individual-level causes (e.g., experience, education, occupational choice).  
- Employer-reported data may have reporting bias (only organizations above a reporting threshold are required to declare).  
- Bonus pay data can be skewed by outliers and may not reflect base pay disparities.  
- The data does not fully capture intersectional effects (e.g., race, age, disability) that may influence pay gap dynamics.
